A Tribute To

Dr. Norman Borlaug

The man who fed the world

The Father of the Green Revolution

Norman Ernest Borlaug (1914–2009) was an American agronomist who grew up on a farm in Iowa. Working in Mexico, he developed high-yielding, disease-resistant, semi-dwarf wheat varieties that helped countries such as Mexico, India and Pakistan dramatically increase their harvests.

His work is widely credited with helping to avert famine for hundreds of millions of people, and in 1970 he was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for his contribution to world food supply.

“Food is the moral right of all who are born into this world.” — attributed to Norman Borlaug

Life & Legacy

  1. 1914

    Born on March 25 near Cresco, Iowa, and raised on the family farm.

  2. 1940s

    Begins agricultural research in Mexico, starting the wheat breeding program that would change global farming.

  3. 1960s

    His high-yield wheat varieties spread across South Asia, helping nations avoid widespread hunger.

  4. 1970

    Awarded the Nobel Peace Prize.

  5. 1977

    Receives the Presidential Medal of Freedom.

  6. 2006

    Receives the Congressional Gold Medal.

  7. 2009

    Passes away on September 12, leaving a legacy of food security and agricultural education.
